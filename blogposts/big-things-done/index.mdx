This book is one of my favourite regaring project planning and management, because it has a lot of very interesting and valuable takeaways on how to handle project planning and how to think about large projects, construction, software or otherwise.

## Haste makes waste

The biggest cost in a project occurs during execution rather than during planning. Therefore good planning is a prerequisite for mitigating risks. This is not a bias against action, but rather a bias in favour of thinking.

**Planning and constraints enable true creativity**. In software engineering these **enabling constraints** could be choosing a well established and opinionated framework. The two factors that hinder productivity are:

-   Feeling that we are not in control
-   Thinking that others are judging us

Planning prevents the feeling of being out of control, therefore enhances creativity.

## Principles of project thinking

Starting from the goal and thinking backwards (right to left thinking principle) helps us to focus on what is important. It helps us to discover alternatives, question everything and primes us to think that there is always more to learn.

There is a cognitive bias: WYSIATI - What You See is All There Is. This is a psychological pitfall an availability bias. When facing a decision about a project the mind tends to loop through familiar events instead of considering alternatives. We literally forget to account for what we don't know. To avoid this bias we have to avoid assumptions, **keep an open mind, actively discover alternatives and engage in different viewpoints**.

## Project strategies

Modularity and simplicity foster repetition based learning and this enhances speed of delivery. Simple things are good and small modular things are simple. Just like with evolution, the most fit easily reproducible modules survive. Having small modules we can experiment and find the right modules that are easily reproducible. **Reproduction in turn leads to repetition which results in learning and accelerated development cycles**. This is important because tacit knowledge is created by repetition. Repetition leads to experience which creates tacit knowledge and **repetition is enabled by modularity**.
